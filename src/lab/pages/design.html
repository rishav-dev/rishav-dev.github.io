Which design should the capstone build?

Six concepts, eleven criteria. Seven criteria come straight from the bench (reliability, size range, pill safety, throughput, repeatability, fill-level robustness, forgiveness of the adjustment); four are engineering judgement and are marked as such, with the reason given. Move the weights, the ranking and its stability are recomputed.

1 · Decision matrix

Scores are 0–10. Computed scores update if the bench data is regenerated; judged scores are the team's to challenge, click a score for its rationale.

Weights

2 · How sure is the ranking?

Each of 2 000 trials multiplies every weight by a random factor between 0.4 and 2.2 and re-ranks the concepts. A concept that wins only under one particular set of weights is a preference, not a result.

3 · Trade-off: no single "best", a frontier

A weighted score always hides a choice about the weights. This is the same six concepts on two objectives that come straight off the bench, with no weighting at all: reliability (dose-exact rate, P1) and pill safety margin (1 − the worst squeeze/impact force as a share of the pill's crush or drop rating, across P1 and the P6 stress test). A concept is Pareto-optimal (filled dot) if no other concept beats it on both axes at once; a concept behind the frontier (hollow dot) is strictly worse than some other concept on both counts, so it needs a reason outside this chart to justify picking it.
