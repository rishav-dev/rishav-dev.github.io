UMass MIE capstone · pill dispensing module

One pill at a time, tested in simulation

The first design had a known weakness, so we did not copy it. This site tests six ways of dispensing exactly one pill from a bottle, a sorting wheel, a vacuum arm, a sliding pocket, a gravity lane, sensor-gated belts and a vacuum disc, in a rigid-body simulation with real masses, friction, motor limits and suction force, then asks the questions the project brief asks: how often is it one pill, how do we know, and does the pill survive?

How the simulation is checked

Real units

Millimetres, milliseconds and grams, so every force comes out in newtons. Pill masses come from size and density, crush strengths from size, drop ratings from real pill classes.

Validated engine

Free fall, projectile, incline sliding, restitution, momentum, actuator force limits and suction force are checked against closed-form answers, live in your browser on the physics check page. One known limitation is documented on the same page.

Jams are real

Motors have force limits. A pill caught in a mechanism stalls the motor at its rated force; that stall is the jam signal and the squeeze the pill sees. Suction is a force limited by pressure × area, so a snagged pill drops.

Repeatable

Every result comes from seeded runs and the raw trial records ship with the site. Re-run any protocol in the browser on the bench page, or regenerate everything with node bench/run.js.
